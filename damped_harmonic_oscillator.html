📚 Physics & Mathematical Principles

1. Undamped Case (c = 0, ζ = 0)

Governed by m x'' + k x = 0. Characteristic roots are pure imaginary r = ± i ω₀.

Oscillates indefinitely at natural angular frequency ω₀ = √(k/m) with strictly constant amplitude x₀. Mechanical energy oscillates between kinetic and elastic potential.

2. Underdamped Case (0 < c < c_c, 0 < ζ < 1)

Roots are complex conjugate r = -γ ± i ω_d, where γ = c/(2m) and ω_d = ω₀√(1 - ζ²).

The oscillator completes oscillations with decreasing amplitude bounded by the exponential envelope ± x₀ e^(-γ t) / √(1 - ζ²). Energy is continuously dissipated as heat through damping.

3. Critical (ζ = 1) vs Overdamped (ζ > 1)

Critical Damping (c = c_c = 2√(mk)): Repeated root r = -ω₀. Returns to equilibrium in the minimal possible time without overshoot (used in car suspensions and galvanometers).

Overdamped (c > c_c): Two distinct negative real roots. Viscous drag dominates, resulting in a sluggish return to equilibrium (e.g. hydraulic door closers).
